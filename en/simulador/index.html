How it corrects you
  • Preflop: the chart library (the same as on the charts page) with the loss in bb of each action for your hand. If an action loses nothing in the chart, it is correct even if the chart mixes it. Below, the adjustment against that type of opponent that came out of the labs (steals, isolating limps, recreational opens, all-ins).
  • Opponents: each type plays with the frequencies measured in 14,000+ real tournament hands: how often it opens, limps, defends or re-raises; how often it c-bets, double-barrels and bets the river; how often it folds by bet size; how often it raises and with what share of bluffs. It picks which hands it plays by strength: the best for value and draws or trash to bluff.
  • Flop, turn and river (the grade): a GTO solver (discounted CFR on flop and turn, CFR+ on the river) computes each street's equilibrium between your range and theirs in theory: the preflop library for both and, after that, the equilibrium itself on earlier streets. The grade is what your play loses against the best action with your exact hand (the EV of each action assumes you keep playing as well as possible against their equilibrium strategy). On the river it is exact for its tree (bets of 33, 75 and 125%, one 3x raise and the all-in re-raise); on the turn it uses 5 sample rivers; on the flop it is an approximation (4 sample turns with one turn bet, and the river as showdown).
  • Against this opponent (the exploit): below, your equity against the range that opponent has left given how they play, how often they will fold and the EV of each option. If it differs from GTO, the difference is what you gain by exploiting their style.
  • Botes a tres: if preflop ends three-way (an open, a call and you call; or you limp behind a limper and the BB checks), flop, turn and river are played three-way. The GTO solver is heads-up, so there the grade is the exploit one: EV against how those two opponents play (with fewer bets and more folds than heads-up), sampling their ranges and the remaining cards.
  • Veredicto: Correct if you lose very little against the best option (with a little more margin if the equilibrium often uses your action with your hand); Inaccuracy if you lose something; Mistake if you lose a lot for the size of the pot.

How it works

  1. Set up your practice (above): tournament format, stack, opponents and what you want to train.
  2. Decide at the table with the buttons or the keys 1-4.
  3. Read the correction on the right: grade, the EV each option loses against GTO and why, and how that opponent plays.
  4. Repeat your mistakes: in “What to practise” pick “My leaks” and the spots that cost you will come up more often.

How the poker simulator works

The simulator deals 6-max tournament hands and, at every decision, compares your play with our solver's equilibrium strategy and with how opponents really play: regulars, aggressive players, maniacs and recreationals measured in 14,000+ real hands. You see at once whether it was correct, an inaccuracy or a mistake, and how many big blinds it costs.

You can choose the format (ChipEV, PKO, bubble, ITM, final table), the stack depth and what to practise. “My leaks” mode repeats the situations you get wrong more often.

Frequently asked questions

Is the simulator free?

Yes, with no sign-up and no real money: it is a training tool.

Does it correct postflop too?

Yes: flop, turn and river are solved in your browser for your exact hand.

What are the opponents like?

Profiles calibrated with thousands of real tournament hands: how often they open, call, fold and bluff.
