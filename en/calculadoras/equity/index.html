How to read the results

Equity is the share of the pot you take on average if nobody bets again: wins plus half the ties. Against a range, the result weights each combination (combos blocked by your cards or the board don't count).

ICM converts chips into money. On the bubble and at the final table, losing chips costs more than winning them is worth: that is why ICM charts fold hands that would go all-in in ChipEV.

Pot odds is the minimum equity to call without losing money. MDF (minimum defence frequency) is how much of your range you must continue with so an opponent's bluff doesn't win automatically.

What each tool calculates

Equity: the probability of winning for a hand or range against one or more ranges, preflop or with cards on the board. It tells you whether an all-in call is worth it or how much your draw is worth.

Pot odds and MDF: how much equity you need to call a bet (bet / final pot) and how often you must defend so an opponent's bluff doesn't make money automatically. ICM: what your chips are worth in money given the payouts left, with the Malmuth-Harville model. Varianza: simulates thousands of tournaments with your ROI to show normal downswings and the bankroll you need.

Frequently asked questions

How are pot odds calculated?

Divide what you have to call by the final pot after your call. If you call 10 into a pot that will be 40, you need to win 25% of the time.

What is ICM?

A model that converts chips into money according to the payouts: on the bubble or at the final table, losing chips costs more than winning them is worth.
