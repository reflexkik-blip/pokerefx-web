How to study poker: a step-by-step plan to improve (free)

A guide to studying tournament poker from scratch or to break out of a plateau: what to study first, how much time, how to memorise ranges, how to review your hands and which free tools to use.

Most players “study” by watching videos, but barely improve. Studying poker works like learning a language: a little every day, practising actively and correcting mistakes as soon as they appear. This is the plan we propose.

1. Start with preflop (weeks 1-4)

Preflop is where the most money is given away in tournaments and the easiest to improve, because the decisions repeat. Priorities:

  1. Opening from each position with 20-40 big blinds: BTN open, CO…
  2. Push/fold with 15 big blinds or fewer: push/fold chart.
  3. Defending the big blind: how much to defend by stack.
  4. The small blind: limp, raise or all-in.

2. Memorise with spaced repetition, not by rereading

Rereading a chart doesn't fix it in memory. Redrawing it from memory does. In the range trainer every range goes through study, trace and draw from memory, and the app asks for it again just before you forget it. Five minutes a day is enough.

3. Practica decisiones completas

Knowing the range is not the same as deciding well at the table against a specific opponent. The simulador deals you tournament hands against opponents with real profiles and corrects every decision, preflop and postflop, with the EV you lose.

4. Review your own hands

After every session, review 3-5 important hands: all-ins, big pots and doubts. Use the hand replayer (Winamax and PokerStars) and check the equity with the calculadora.

5. Understand ICM and the formats

Once you master ChipEV, learn how ranges change in PKO, burbuja and mesa final and in the ICM.

6. Manage variance and bankroll

Even a good player spends months in the red. Work out how many buy-ins you need with the variance simulator and play at a level your bankroll can handle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to study poker?

Combining active memorisation of ranges (spaced repetition), practising decisions with instant correction and reviewing your own hands, about 30 minutes a day.

Where do I start studying tournament poker?

With preflop: open ranges by position, push/fold with few big blinds and big blind defence. It is where most is lost and the fastest to improve.

Can you study poker for free?
