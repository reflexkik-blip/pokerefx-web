Pot odds in poker: how to calculate them and when to call

What pot odds are, how to calculate them in seconds, the equity you need by bet size, MDF and how to combine them with your outs. With a table and examples.

pot odds answer one question: how often do I have to win for calling to be profitable? If the answer is lower than your real chance of winning (your equity), you call; if it is higher, you fold.

The formula

Equity needed = what you call ÷ the final pot (the pot after your call). Example: the pot is 100, the opponent bets 50 and you call 50. The final pot is 200, so you need to win 50/200 = 25% of the time.

MDF (minimum defence frequency) is the other side: if you fold more than that, any opponent bluff makes money automatically.

Combinar pot odds and outs

With a flush draw on the flop you have 9 outs: you hit on the turn 19% of the time. Against a half-pot bet you need 25%: the next card alone is not enough, although the implied odds (what you will win later if you hit) can make up for it. If the opponent goes all-in, count both cards: 35%, and you call. The odds of every draw are in the guide on poker probabilities.

Pot odds en torneos (preflop)

In the big blind you already have 1 big blind in and the pot holds blinds and ante, so you call at an excellent price. That is why, against the button's all-in with 12 big blinds, the big blind only needs to win 43% and calls with 31% of its hands. Every range page shows the pot odds of its spot.

Frequently asked questions

How are pot odds calculated?

Divide what you have to call by the final pot after your call. Calling 50 into a pot that will be 200 requires winning 25% of the time.

What is MDF in poker?

Minimum defence frequency: the percentage of your range you must continue with so the opponent's bluffs don't win automatically. Against a pot-sized bet it is 50%.

What are implied odds?

The chips you expect to win on later streets if you hit your draw; they let you call with less equity than the pot odds ask for.
