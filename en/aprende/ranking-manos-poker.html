Poker hand rankings: order of hands and probabilities

The order of poker hands from highest to lowest (royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush…) with the exact probability of making each one in Texas Hold'em and how ties are decided.

In Texas Hold'em each player makes the best five-card combination with their two cards and the five on the board. This is the ranking, from highest to lowest, with the exact probability of ending with each hand using all seven cards (calculated over all 133,784,560 possible combinations).

Notice something that surprises almost everyone: with seven cards it is more likely to end with two pair (23.5%) than with high card (17.4%), and the most frequent hand is one pair (43.8%). That is why a lone pair rarely wins a big pot.

How ties are decided

If two players have the same hand, the higher cards win. With the same pair, the kicker (the highest side card) decides: A-K beats A-Q on an A-7-2 flop. In a flush the highest card of the flush wins; in a straight, the highest card of the straight (A-2-3-4-5, the “wheel”, is the lowest). Suits never break ties: if all five cards are equal, the pot is split.

Common mistakes reading the board

1) Forgetting that the board can “play” for you: if the board is a straight and nobody improves it, the pot is split. 2) Counting six cards: only five count. 3) Thinking a flush needs both cards in your hand to be suited: one is enough if there are four of that suit on the board.

Frequently asked questions

What wins, a flush or a straight?

The flush beats the straight. It is less likely: 3.0% vs 4.6% with seven cards.

What wins, a full house or a flush?

The full house (three of a kind plus a pair) beats the flush.

What is the highest hand in poker?

The royal flush: A-K-Q-J-10 of the same suit. It comes once every 30,940 hands seen to the river.

Do suits have an order in Texas Hold'em?

No. No suit is worth more than another: if two hands are identical, the pot is split.
