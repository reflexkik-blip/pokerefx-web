Poker probabilities: outs, draws and starting hands

The poker probabilities used most at the table: how often you get aces or a pair, flop a set or a flush, complete a draw with your outs, the rule of 2 and 4, and the most famous matchups.

You don't need to be a mathematician to play well, but you do need to know a dozen numbers. These are the ones that change the most decisions, all calculated exactly.

On the flop

What you are looking forChance on the flop
A set or better with a pocket pair11.8% (1 in 8.5)
A flush with two suited cards0.84% (1 in 118)
Flush draw (two of the suit on the board)10.9%

That is why calling “to hit a set” only pays if the opponent has plenty of chips behind: what you win the time it comes in has to pay for the seven or eight times it doesn't.

A shortcut for the table: multiply your outs by 4 on the flop (if you will see two cards) and by 2 on the turn. With 9 outs: about 36% and 18%, very close to the exact values. Compare it with the pot odds to know whether to call.

Frequently asked questions

How often do you get aces?

Once every 221 hands (0.45%).

What is the rule of 2 and 4?

An approximation: your outs times 4 give the chance of hitting from flop to river; times 2, the chance of hitting on a single card.

How often do you flop a set with a pair?

On the flop, 11.8%: once every 8.5 times.

Is A-K a favourite against a small pair?

No: against 2-2 it wins 47%; the pair is a slight favourite.
