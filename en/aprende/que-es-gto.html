What GTO is and how to use it in tournaments

GTO (“game theory optimal”) is an equilibrium strategy: if you play it, no opponent can beat you in the long run by changing theirs. It is not the one that wins most against a specific opponent; it is the one that loses least against the worst possible opponent.

Why it matters in tournaments

In an MTT you sit with players you don't know. The equilibrium is the safe starting point: it tells you which hands to open, how much to defend and how often to bet so you can't be exploited while you learn how the table plays.

Theory and exploitation, together

Real opponents deviate: many fold too much to the second barrel, others call too much on the river. When you know how far a type of player deviates, you adjust: more bluffs against those who fold, more value against those who call. The PokerEFX simulator shows you both grades at every decision: the equilibrium one and the EV against that opponent.

How to practise it

  1. Learn the preflop charts for your format (ChipEV, PKO, ICM).
  2. Play hands in the simulator and look at how much EV each mistake loses, not just whether you got it right.
  3. Repeat the spots that cost you most with “My leaks” mode.
