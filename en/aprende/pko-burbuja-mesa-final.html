Ranges in PKO, on the bubble and at the final table compared with ChipEV

How tournament preflop ranges change with bounties (PKO), on the bubble and at the final table: how much to open and what to call an all-in with, depending on whether you cover or are covered. Solver data.

ChipEV charts assume every chip is worth the same. That is not true in real tournaments: the recompensas of a PKO pay you for knockouts, and the ICM of the bubble and the final table punishes losing chips. We solved the same preflop tree in each format; these are the most important changes.

  • PKO: you call quite a lot more (the bounty is extra money if you win).
  • Bubble: you call far less: losing the all-in knocks you out of the money. It is the biggest change in all of tournament poker.
  • Final table: halfway, because pay jumps matter but you are already in the money.

When you cover a todos, ICM plays in your favour: opponents can't call you without risking their tournament, so you open much more (on the bubble, up to 70% of hands with 14 big blinds). When you are covered, you open less than in ChipEV.

How to use it

  • Before every decision, ask yourself: who covers whom? It changes the range more than position does.
  • In PKO, look at the bounty size: the bigger it is, the more you call.
  • Check the eleven versions in the interactive chart and train them in the simulador, which has every format.

Frequently asked questions

Why do you call less on the bubble?

Because of ICM: losing an all-in on the bubble leaves you without a payout, and that costs more than you gain by doubling your chips. With the same stack, the BB calls an all-in far less often than in ChipEV.

How do ranges change in PKO?

In progressive bounty tournaments you call and open more than in ChipEV when you cover the opponent, because winning the all-in also gives you their bounty.
