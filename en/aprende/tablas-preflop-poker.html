Poker preflop charts for tournaments: the complete guide

How to read and use poker preflop charts in tournaments: what percentage to open from each position by your big blinds, how they change with the stack, and links to hundreds of free GTO ranges.

A preflop chart tells you what to do with each of the 169 starting hands in a given situation: your position, your stack and what the opponents did before you. In tournaments they are the base of everything: most expensive mistakes are made before the flop.

How to read a 13×13 chart

Pairs sit on the diagonal (AA top left, 22 bottom right). Above the diagonal are the suited hands (same suit, such as AKs) and below, the offsuit (AKo). Each colour is an action: raise, call, limp, all-in or fold. If a cell has two colours, the equilibrium mixes both actions.

Three ideas that come out of the table: the later you act, the more hands you open (on the button, more than half with deep stacks); with fewer big blinds you open a little less but more often all-in; and the SB plays a huge number of hands because it only has to beat the BB, almost always by limping.

The situations that matter most

  • Abrir (RFI): nadie ha entrado. Example: BTN with 20 bb.
  • Contra open: call, re-raise or fold. BB vs BTN with 25 bb.
  • Contra all-in: the range to call a push. BB vs SB with 10 bb.
  • SB limps: how to attack the limp from the BB and what the SB does when the BB raises.

ChipEV, ICM and PKO

The charts above are ChipEV: every chip is worth the same. Near the bubble or at the final table ICM forces tighter play, and in bounty tournaments (PKO) you call more because you win the bounty. The interactive chart has eleven formats with all those cases.

Frequently asked questions

What is a preflop chart?

A 13×13 grid that tells you which action to take with each starting hand in a given situation (position, stack and previous action).

How many hands should I open from the button?

In a 6-max ChipEV tournament, the equilibrium opens about 40-50% of hands from the button depending on the stack.

Do the charts work for ICM?

ChipEV charts don't; with ICM (bubble, final table) you have to play tighter. The interactive chart includes ICM and PKO formats.
