ICM and the bubble: why fold hands that would go all-in

In a tournament you are paid by finishing place, not by chips. Doubling your stack doesn't double what you will cash, but losing it all leaves you with nothing. That asymmetry is ICM.

An example with numbers

With three players on 5,000, 3,000 and 2,000 chips and payouts of 50, 30 and 20, the leader has 50% of the chips but their ICM value is 38.4: less than their share. The short stack has 20% of the chips and is worth 28.9. Check it in the ICM calculator.

What changes in your game

  • You call all-ins with a much narrower range, especially against stacks that cover you.
  • Big stacks can put pressure on medium ones, who lose the most if they get it wrong.
  • Short stacks gain value by surviving: sometimes folding beats a small edge.
