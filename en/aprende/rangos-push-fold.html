Push/fold ranges: all-in chart by position and big blinds

Push/fold chart for poker tournaments: what percentage of hands to go all-in with from each position with 5 to 16 big blinds and what to call the all-in with from the BB. Free and computed with a solver.

With few big blinds poker becomes simpler: almost every decision is all-in or fold. Raising and folding leaves too many chips in the pot, and limping only works from the SB. This table sums up the push/fold equilibrium in a 6-max tournament (ChipEV, 1 bb ante in the BB).

The last column is the range the BB calls the SB's all-in with: the shorter the stack, the wider the call, because the pot already gives you a very good price.

How to use it

  • Count your effective big blinds: the smaller stack between yours and that of whoever can call you.
  • With 10-14 bb the equilibrium mixes some small opens with strong, playable hands; with 8 or fewer, almost everything is all-in.
  • From the SB limping gains weight from 10 bb up: see the small blind guide.

Practise it until it is automatic: in the simulador pick stacks of 5 to 15 bb.

Frequently asked questions

With how many big blinds should you play push/fold?

Below about 10-12 big blinds almost every decision is all-in or fold; between 12 and 20 small opens and all-ins mix.

Which hands to go all-in with from the button with 10 bb?

According to the ChipEV equilibrium, about 44% of hands; the full chart is on the BTN open page with 10 bb.
