Poker glossary: 70+ terms explained, with a quiz

What RFI, 3-bet, squeeze, ICM, ChipEV, MDF, c-bet, limp, iso-raise, PKO and other tournament poker terms mean, explained in one sentence with examples.

75 tournament poker terms explained in one sentence. At the end, a quick quiz to check you know them.

3-bet, 4-bet
The first re-raise and the next one.
All-in (push, shove)
Betting all your chips. With few big blinds it is the main play: see push/fold.
Ante
A forced bet added to the pot before the deal; in many tournaments only the big blind pays it (big blind ante).
Backdoor
A draw that needs two cards in a row (turn and river) to complete.
Bankroll
Money set aside only for poker. Its size is measured in buy-ins; work out yours with the variance simulator.
bb (big blinds grandes)
A unit to measure stacks and winnings: 20 bb is 20 times the big blind.
BB, SB, BTN, CO, HJ, UTG
Positions at a 6-max table: big blind, small blind, button, cutoff, hijack and under the gun.
Blocker
A card in your hand that reduces the opponent's strong combinations (for example, an ace blocks AA and AK).
Bluff catcher
A hand that only beats the opponent's bluffs: it is no good for value-betting, but it is good for calling.
Bote multiway
A pot with three or more players; you bluff less and bet more for value.
Bounty
The reward for knocking out a player in KO or PKO tournaments.
Bubble
The stage of the tournament just before the money; ICM forces tighter play.
Buy-in
The entry price of a tournament.
C-bet
Continuation bet: the preflop raiser bets the flop. See c-bet by flop.
Call
Matching the opponent's bet.
Check
Passing without betting when nobody has bet before on the street.
Check-raise
Checking and, when the opponent bets, raising.
ChipEV
A model in which every chip is worth the same; it applies early in the tournament, far from the money.
Coinflip
An all-in where both hands have close to 50% equity, such as a pair against two overcards.
Cold call
Calling a raise without having put money in the pot before.
Combo
Each specific combination of a hand: a pair has 6 combos, a suited hand 4 and an offsuit hand 12.
Continuation bet
Ver C-bet.
Cooler
A hand where you lose a lot with a very strong hand against an even better one, with nobody making a mistake.
Donk bet
Betting out of position into the player who had the initiative on the previous street.
Draw (proyecto)
A hand that is not made yet but can make a flush or a straight.
Effective stack
The smallest stack among the players involved: it is the most that can be won or lost in the hand.
Equity
The share of the pot that is yours according to your chances of winning. It is calculated with the equity calculator.
EV (valor esperado)
What a play wins or loses on average. The charts show how many bb each action loses against the best one.
Fish (recreational)
An amateur player who makes big mistakes; the laboratorio shows how they play.
Float
Calling a bet with a weak hand to bluff on a later street.
Flop, turn, river
The three postflop streets: three community cards, the fourth and the fifth.
Fold equity
The part of your winnings that comes from the opponent folding to your bet.
GTO
An equilibrium strategy that cannot be exploited. See what GTO is.
Gutshot
Gutshot straight draw: only four cards complete it.
Hero call
Calling the river with a weak hand thinking the opponent is bluffing.
ICM
A model that converts chips into money according to the payouts; it forces you to risk less on the bubble. See ICM and burbuja.
Implied odds
What you expect to win on future streets if you hit your draw, on top of the current pot.
In position (IP)
Acting after the opponent on the postflop streets: a big advantage.
Iso-raise
Raising against a limp to play the pot against a single opponent and with the initiative.
ITM
«In the money»: estar en premios.
Kicker
The side card that breaks ties between hands of the same rank.
LAG
A loose-aggressive player: plays many hands and bets a lot.
Limp
Entering the pot by just calling the big blind. From the SB it is a GTO play; see the SB guide.
Limp-raise / limp-shove
Limping and re-raising (or going all-in) when someone raises behind.
MDF
Minimum defence frequency: the part of your range you must continue with so the opponent's bluff doesn't win automatically.
Final table
The last players in a tournament, where pay jumps are biggest and ICM weighs most.
Min-raise
A raise to twice the previous bet; common for opening with mid stacks.
Nash (push/fold)
The all-in or fold equilibrium with short stacks; see how we verify it.
Nuts
The best possible hand on that board.
Offsuit (o)
Two cards of different suits, such as AKo.
Open raise (RFI)
The first raise of the pot when everyone has folded before.
Out of position (OOP)
Acting before the opponent on the postflop streets.
Overbet
A bet bigger than the pot.
Overcard
A card higher than those on the board or than the opponent's pair.
PKO
A progressive bounty tournament: you win a bounty for each opponent you knock out.
Polarizado
A range made of very strong hands and bluffs, with no medium hands.
Position
The order of action; the later, the better.
Pot odds
The ratio between what you call and what you can win; it tells you the minimum equity to call.
Probe bet
Betting the turn out of position when the opponent checked the flop.
Rake
The fee the poker site charges.
Rango
The set of hands someone takes an action with, such as “22+, A2s+, KTo+”.
Reg
A regular player, a frequent player in those tournaments with a technical base.
Reshove
Going all-in after someone has opened or gone all-in.
ROI
Return on investment: what you win on average for each euro of buy-in.
Semifarol
Betting with a draw that can improve to the best hand if you are called.
Set
Three of a kind made with a pocket pair and one card on the board.
Showdown
The moment the cards are shown at the end of the hand.
SPR
Stack-to-pot ratio at the start of the flop; it shows how much play is left.
Squeeze
Re-raising (3-bet) when there is an open and one or more calls before you.
Stack
A player's chips.
Suited (s)
Two cards of the same suit, such as AKs.
Tilt
Playing worse out of frustration after bad hands.
Value bet
A bet to get called by a worse hand.
VPIP / PFR
HUD stats: the percentage of hands an opponent plays and the percentage they open by raising.
Wet / dry board
A board with many or few possible draws; it changes the c-bet size.
